# What Rampart still needs for these policies to be exact

Building the policies and the frameworks showed where the server cannot yet say what a standard says. Each item is something that was hit while writing them, with the evidence, so it can be decided on its merits. Size is a rough guess: S is a field or a check, M is a feature, L is a design.

| # | What | What was hit | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Import frameworks from official catalogues** | NIST publishes SP 800-53, 800-171 and others as OSCAL JSON. Writing ids by hand risks a typo; reading them from the catalogue does not. A `rampart` command that takes an OSCAL catalogue, a list of control ids and a mapping file (weaknesses and kinds of scan per control) would make the identifiers and titles the publisher's by construction, and the mapping the only part that is ours. This repository checked its NIST ids by hand against the catalogue; an importer would make that automatic. | M |
| 2 | **Controls that read time and threat** | A control can match only weaknesses, kinds of scan, rules and a severity floor. It cannot say "on the CISA KEV list" (CISA BOD 22-01), "past its SLA due date" or "open longer than N days" (PCI DSS 6.3.3, NIST SI-2 and 800-171 03.14.01, CRA Annex I II.2 "without delay", FedRAMP). Today these are approximated by "an open high finding", which fails a finding that is a day old. `match.kev`, `match.overdue` and `match.olderThanDays` would express them exactly, using data Rampart already holds. | M |
| 3 | **Controls that only need evidence** | The server refuses a control with `evidence` and nothing else (`give match.cwes, match.categories or match.rules, or set manual: true`). "An SBOM exists for every application" (NIST CM-8, PCI 6.3.2, CRA II.1, CSF ID.AM-02, NIS2 21.2.d) and "a scan ran this quarter" (PCI 11.3.1) are evidence statements with nothing to match. They are manual today. An evidence kind for SBOM presence, and evidence-only controls, would turn them green when true. | S |
| 4 | **Control ids with parentheses** | The id pattern is `^[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9._:-]{0,63}$`, so `IA-5(7)` is written `IA-5.7` and `164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A)` as `164.308.a.1.ii.A`. A separate `displayId` would let the screen show the standard's own form. | S |
| 5 | **An evidence window per control** | One `evidenceWindowDays` applies to a whole framework (1 to 366). PCI DSS wants 90 days for scans and 12 months for penetration tests; HIPAA, ISO and CRA are mixed. | S |
| 6 | **More facts for a policy to read** | A risk policy reads `finding.*`, `vuln.{id,kev,epss,exploited,exploit_maturity}` and four fields of the application. It can now read the application's environment and type (`app.environment`, `app.type`), but it still cannot read `complianceScope` or tags, nor the CVSS vector, the EPSS percentile, the KEV due date, whether a fix exists, or reachability. The CVSS score is already stored with the vulnerability and the fixed version with the finding's location. A policy that says "a PCI-scoped application weighs 50 more" is impossible today. | M |
| 7 | **Provenance on the policy itself** | A factor has `reason` text but no field that says *why this number*. A `basis` (standard, convention, ours) and a `reference` per factor and per SLA profile, returned by `GET /api/v1/scoring` and shown on the page that explains the score, would carry the reasoning with the number. [SCORING.md](SCORING.md) holds it for now. | S |
| 8 | **Reproducible scores** | A score is stored with its band; to say which policy produced it, and show its breakdown a year later, the policy version and a snapshot of the factors have to be stored with it. The chart already stamps `rampart.aspm.dev/policy-set-version` on every policy. | M |
| 9 | **Apply-time checks that catch silent mistakes** | (a) `Days()` caps every deadline at `maxDays`, so a policy with a matrix cell above `maxDays` is silently shortened: `deploy/presets/clusterslapolicy-pci-dss.yaml` says 90 for medium and low and sets `maxDays: 30`, which makes every cell 30. (b) `deploy/presets/clusterriskpolicy-default.yaml` lists 8 factors and omits APP_HIGH, EXPLOITED_REPORTED, EXPLOIT_WEAPONIZED and SECRET_VERIFIED; because a policy that lists factors replaces the built-in list, applying it removes those four. (c) A factor whose condition matches no application and no finding ("prd" for "production") says nothing. A warning for each, and a test that every preset equals what its comment says, would catch all three. | S |
| 10 | **An explicit link from a tenant to its policy** | The policy in force is found by name: the namespaced one named `default`, else the first by name, else the cluster one. Nothing prevents two, and a team that can write to its own namespace can name a policy `default`. `Tenant.spec.riskPolicy` and `slaPolicy`, validated when applied, would make the choice visible and administrator-owned. | M |
| 11 | **Option lists for context values** | `environment`, `dataClassification` and `compensatingControls` are fixed enumerations in the resource schema, and the policy sees the stored spelling (`api_gateway`), not the resource's (`apiGateway`), so a wrong literal in a factor never matches and nothing says so. Tenant-defined options, validated against the policy, would remove both. | M |
| 12 | **Calibration** | The band limits and every point value are Ours, not validated against outcomes. Rampart holds what is needed to measure them: the score of a finding when it was seen, and whether the vulnerability was later added to KEV or exploited. An export of score against later exploitation, per band, would show whether 800 is a good place for the critical band. | M |
| 13 | **Crosswalk and evidence export** | One finding bears on controls in several frameworks, and an assessor wants the evidence as a file. A per-control export (CSV, and OSCAL assessment results, which NIST defines) would serve both. | M |

## How these relate to the rules built on top

The scoring and SLA policies in this repository are the foundation the rules are built on. Items 2, 3 and 6 decide how exact the rules can be; items 7 and 8 decide whether a score can be defended afterwards; items 9 and 10 decide whether a wrong policy can be applied without anyone noticing. None blocks this repository: everything here works with Rampart as it is, and says where it approximates.
