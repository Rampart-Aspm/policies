# NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5: vulnerability and supply-chain subset

_Generated by `scripts/generate.py` from `frameworks/nist-800-53-r5.yaml`. Do not edit this file._

The controls of the US federal security catalogue (the base of FedRAMP) that software scanning can speak to: risk and vulnerability management, flaw remediation, developer testing, secrets, cryptography, input validation and supply chain.

## Where it comes from

- **Resource:** `nist-800-53-r5` (a `ComplianceFramework`), switched on with `compliance.nist80053.enabled`. It reads only applications that list `nist-800-53` in `complianceScope`.
- **Version covered:** Rev. 5
- **Source:** <https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/final>
- **Logo:** <https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Rampart-Aspm/policies/main/logos/nist-800-53-r5.svg>, a mark Rampart draws itself (a flat shape with the framework's letters), not the publisher's logo; see [../METHOD.md](../METHOD.md), "Logos".
- **Verification: primary.** Identifiers and titles were read from the publisher's own text or machine-readable data.
  - How: Every id and title was checked against NIST's OSCAL catalogue of Rev. 5 (usnistgov/oscal-content, SP 800-53 Rev. 5 JSON). All 15 are present with exactly the titles shown.
- **Licence and wording:** NIST publications are in the public domain, so the published titles are used as they are.

## How it was written

The general method is in [../METHOD.md](../METHOD.md): which controls are chosen, how a control is matched to scanner findings or left to a person, and what the severity floors and evidence windows mean. For this framework:

- Only controls that scanning, or the absence of it, can speak to were chosen: risk and vulnerability management (RA-3, RA-5, SI-2), development and testing (SA-11, SA-15, CA-8), supply chain and inventory (SR-3, SR-4, CM-8), configuration (CM-6), and technical protections that have well-known weakness classes (IA-5(7), SC-8, SC-13, SC-28, SI-10). The catalogue has about a thousand controls and enhancements; the rest (physical, personnel, incident response, contingency planning) give a scanner nothing to say.
- RA-5 and SI-2 read open findings of severity high or above. The catalogue leaves the timeframe to the organisation, and FedRAMP, built on it, gives critical and high a deadline. The deadline itself is the SLA policy's job, not this framework's.
- Weakness lists for SC-8, SC-13, SC-28 and SI-10 are ours: NIST does not map its controls to CWE. They are the usual weakness classes for the property the control protects (see METHOD.md).
- Evidence window: **30 days.** FedRAMP, which is built on this catalogue, asks for vulnerability scans every month.
- 15 controls: 11 matched to scanner findings, 4 manual.

## Controls

| Id | Control | How Rampart reads it | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| RA-3 | Risk Assessment | **Manual.** A person attests it. | An assessment of the organisation's risks is done by people. |
| RA-5 | Vulnerability Monitoring and Scanning | Counts open findings of kind `sca`, `container`, `sast`, `dast`, `iac`, severity **high** or above; evidenced by a scan of kind `sca`, `container`, `sast`, `dast`. | The control is that vulnerability scanning happens and its findings are acted on: any scan of the kinds that find vulnerabilities evidences it, and open high or critical findings count against it. |
| SI-2 | Flaw Remediation | Counts open findings of kind `sca`, `container`, severity **high** or above. | Flaws in other people's code (dependencies and images) are what flaw remediation means for an application; open high or critical ones are the unremediated flaws. |
| SA-11 | Developer Testing and Evaluation | Counts open findings of kind `sast`, severity **high** or above; evidenced by a scan of kind `sast`, `dast`. | Developer testing is static or dynamic testing of the application's own code; open high or critical findings from it count. |
| SA-15 | Development Process, Standards, and Tools | **Manual.** A person attests it. | A development process and its standards are a practice, not something a scan sees. |
| SR-3 | Supply Chain Controls and Processes | **Manual.** A person attests it. | Processes with suppliers are contractual and organisational. |
| SR-4 | Provenance | Counts open findings of kind `supply_chain`; evidenced by a scan of kind `supply_chain`, `sca`. | Findings of kind supply_chain are provenance problems (for example a malicious or tampered package); an SBOM or dependency scan evidences that components are tracked. |

| SC-28 | Protection of Information at Rest | Counts open findings with weakness CWE-311, CWE-312, CWE-313; evidenced by a code scan (`sast` or `dast`), the server's rule for weakness-only controls. | The weaknesses that leave stored data unencrypted. |
| SI-10 | Information Input Validation | Counts open findings with weakness CWE-20, CWE-22, CWE-77, CWE-78, CWE-79, CWE-89, CWE-94, CWE-502, CWE-611, CWE-918; evidenced by a code scan (`sast` or `dast`), the server's rule for weakness-only controls. | Improper input validation and the injection classes that follow from it (path traversal, command, OS command, cross-site scripting, SQL, code injection, deserialization, XML external entities, server-side request forgery). |

## What it does not cover

- No control here measures time. SI-2 asks for flaws to be corrected within an organisation-defined period, and this reads only whether a high or critical finding is open (docs/FOUNDATION.md, item 2).
- Controls outside software (physical, personnel, incident response, contingency planning and the like) are not listed.

## How to read the result

A matched control reads `clear` when a scan that could evidence it ran within the window and nothing it counts is open, `open` when something is open, and `not assessed` when no such scan ran. A manual control stays unassessed until a person attests it. A `clear` control means *no open finding of the kind the control watches*; it does not prove the requirement is met.
