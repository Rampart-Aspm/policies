## How it was written

The general method is in [../METHOD.md](../METHOD.md): which controls are chosen, how a control is matched to scanner findings or left to a person, and what the severity floors and evidence windows mean. For this framework:

- Only the standards that bear on software were chosen (administrative: 164.308(a)(1), (a)(5), (a)(8); technical: 164.312). Physical safeguards, workforce, contingency planning and the organisational requirements are not software findings.
- The Security Rule is technology-neutral and names no weakness, so the weakness lists for encryption, integrity, authentication and transmission are ours (METHOD.md).
- 164.308(a)(8), the periodic evaluation, reads as: tested lately, and nothing critical left open, a deliberate and weaker reading.
- Evidence window: **30 days.** A scan older than this does not evidence a control.
- 9 controls: 8 matched to scanner findings, 1 manual.

## What it does not cover

- The rule is risk-based: an addressable specification can be met by an equivalent measure or documented as not reasonable. A finding against it is evidence to weigh, not a violation.
- Audit controls, contingency planning, physical safeguards and the organisational and documentation requirements are not listed.

## How to read the result

A matched control reads `clear` when a scan that could evidence it ran within the window and nothing it counts is open, `open` when something is open, and `not assessed` when no such scan ran. A manual control stays unassessed until a person attests it. A `clear` control means *no open finding of the kind the control watches*; it does not prove the requirement is met.
