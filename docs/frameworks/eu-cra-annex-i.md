# EU Cyber Resilience Act: Annex I essential cybersecurity requirements

## How it was written

The general method is in [../METHOD.md](../METHOD.md): which controls are chosen, how a control is matched to scanner findings or left to a person, and what the severity floors and evidence windows mean. For this framework:

- Part II is the part about vulnerability handling and is listed in full (all eight points); Part I is listed only for the points a scan can speak to (2(a) to (f)) and for attack surface (2(j)).
- I.2.a asks for products 'without known exploitable vulnerabilities'. Rampart reads open high and critical findings in components. Whether a vulnerability is known to be exploitable is the CISA KEV list, which a control cannot test yet (docs/FOUNDATION.md, item 2).
- II.2 asks for vulnerabilities to be remediated 'without delay'. Time is not testable in a control today (docs/FOUNDATION.md, item 2); the SLA policy sets the deadlines.
- Evidence window: **30 days.** A scan older than this does not evidence a control.
- 15 controls: 7 matched to scanner findings, 8 manual.

## What it does not cover

- The Regulation also covers conformity assessment, reporting of actively exploited vulnerabilities and the duties of importers and distributors; none is a scanner finding.
- Whether a product is in scope, and the dates from which each obligation applies, are for the manufacturer to establish from the Regulation.

## How to read the result

A matched control reads `clear` when a scan that could evidence it ran within the window and nothing it counts is open, `open` when something is open, and `not assessed` when no such scan ran. A manual control stays unassessed until a person attests it. A `clear` control means *no open finding of the kind the control watches*; it does not prove the requirement is met.
