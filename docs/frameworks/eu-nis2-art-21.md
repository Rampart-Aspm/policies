## How it was written

The general method is in [../METHOD.md](../METHOD.md): which controls are chosen, how a control is matched to scanner findings or left to a person, and what the severity floors and evidence windows mean. For this framework:

- All ten points are listed, because Article 21(2) is a short list that an entity has to answer in full; only (e), (f) and (h) have anything a scanner can see.
- (e) is the one that names vulnerability handling and secure development: it reads open high or critical findings. (f) reads as: tested lately, and nothing critical left open, a deliberate and weaker reading. (h) reads the weakness classes of cryptography itself.
- Evidence window: **30 days.** A scan older than this does not evidence a control.
- 10 controls: 3 matched to scanner findings, 7 manual.

## What it does not cover

- The Directive names no weakness; the weakness list for (h) is ours (METHOD.md).
- Reporting obligations (Article 23) and supervision are not listed.

## How to read the result

A matched control reads `clear` when a scan that could evidence it ran within the window and nothing it counts is open, `open` when something is open, and `not assessed` when no such scan ran. A manual control stays unassessed until a person attests it. A `clear` control means *no open finding of the kind the control watches*; it does not prove the requirement is met.
